# Free data for the Options, Futures and Crypto tabs

The repository holds no option quotes, no futures contract prices and no multi-exchange crypto quotes, so those three tabs start on a model and say so. Each of them has a **Real data (free)** card in its left panel that downloads the real thing from public sources that need no account and no key (and, for more futures and for a coin's daily history, from iTick, which needs your key), saves it on your machine and switches the tab to it. You do not find, clean or format anything.

## How to use it

1. Open the Options, Futures or Crypto tab. The card is open while nothing has been downloaded.
2. Tick what you want (tickers, futures roots, coins) and press **Download real data**. A line under the button counts the seconds and a hint beside it estimates the time (ten tickers or roots take a minute or two), because the providers are asked politely, one request after another.
3. The result lists each item with a tick or a cross and the reason for the cross. The tab selects the first download, the badge above every table reads **Downloaded: free data** (or **Mixed sources** when part of the page is still a model, and the parts are listed beside it), and the "How this was made" note names the provider, the time of the download and what the data is not.
4. **Update** repeats a download (the same request, with fresh prices); **Delete** removes it (it asks twice). A futures curve download is merged with the earlier one, so the contracts that have expired since keep their history; an iTick download is topped up with the newest bars, which is one call.

Every download is a file you can open and a `.json` record beside it (`source`, the address, `fetched_at`, `as_of`, the number of rows, the notes, and the request that repeats it). Nothing is written anywhere else: `data/user/options/`, `data/user/futures/` and `data/user/crypto/` are for your own files, which always win over a download of the same name.

## iTick: any futures product, a coin's history, an underlying's prices

[iTick](https://docs.itick.org/) is the data source of the Backtest tab's *Download other tickers from* menu, and the three cards reuse it: the same key (the `ITICK_API_KEY` secret, see [the dashboard guide](dashboard.md#keeping-the-itick-key-safe)), the same provider, the same limit of calls a minute (the plan's, remembered on disk) and the same refusal to spend more than `ITICK_MAX_CALLS_PER_RUN` calls in one run. A card with iTick chosen and no key shows the steps that turn it on; a request that needs more calls than allowed is refused with its cost before any is spent; a rejected key or plan stops the run at once.

What iTick has, and what it does not:

- **Futures: one price series per product, not per contract month.** `NQ` is the E-mini Nasdaq-100 whichever quarter is the front one. The card searches a copy of iTick's own product list (about 1,600 products: 1,381 in the United States, 183 in Hong Kong, 52 in mainland China; the copy is bundled, so searching costs no call), shows the products people look for first, and accepts `US:CODE`, `HK:CODE` or `CN:CODE` typed by hand for one the copy lacks. There is therefore **no curve, roll, carry, basis or open interest** from iTick: the Curve, Roll, Carry and Margin views stay on Yahoo's curves, and an iTick product is read in the **History** view (returns over a day, a month, a quarter and a year, volatility, the worst fall, the calendar months, and the eight largest one-day moves). iTick does not document how its series is joined when one contract expires and the next becomes the front, nor whether its prices are adjusted, so the History view lists the largest moves for you to compare with the roll dates, and the page says that a step that large is often a contract change, not the market.
- **Crypto: a coin's daily closes against USDT, spot only.** iTick has no perpetual prices, funding rates or order books; the exchanges have those. Which exchange's prices its region code stands for is not documented. A coin the exchanges do not list can still be had this way, and iTick's own list of about 220 coins is checked before a call is spent.
- **Options: none.** iTick has no option data at all, and the card says so. What it can add is the underlying's daily prices (Yahoo Finance can too), which give a downloaded chain a realised volatility for any ticker the platform's own price data does not hold; the Options tab then shows realised against implied volatility for it.
- **Never called with a working key by the author.** The requests and answers follow iTick's published documentation, the tests drive the provider through a fake transport, and the client checks what comes back instead of trusting it (bars about a day apart, a history that reaches back as far as asked). What was checked against the live service, without a key: it has `kline` routes for stocks, indices, forex, crypto and `future` (not `futures`) and none for options, it reads the key from a header named `token` (a made-up key is answered "Invalid API key", any other header name "Missing API key"), and the client turns that answer into its plain refusal. What an answer with data looks like is taken from the documentation, not seen. **Test the iTick connection** (in the Backtest tab and, once a key is set, in each card) spends one call and shows what your account returns. The daily bar of a futures product is dated by the exchange's trading day as the app reads it: a bar stamped on the evening before (the day opens at 18:00 in New York) is moved to the next weekday. If your account stamps its bars differently, the dates can be a day off; the History view says nothing about days of the week beyond that.

## What the tabs do with it

- **Options.** The downloaded chain is analysed exactly like a model chain: smile, skew, term structure, surface, Greeks and risk, the strategy builder priced off real quotes. Because a vendor chain gives prices but not the rate and the dividend yield they were struck with, the page assumes the risk-free rate (4% a year, stored with the download) and reads **each expiry's dividend yield from put-call parity at the money**: the forward the market itself implies. That keeps the implied volatilities and the parity checks consistent. Deribit publishes each expiry's forward, which is read directly. The arbitrage view checks only quotes that have a live bid and lie within 5% of the forward for stock and ETF options (15% for the European-style index, Bitcoin and Ether chains): a delayed, wide quote far out of the money, or an in-the-money American option that is worth more than parity says, would otherwise be reported as a violation. Stock and ETF options can be exercised early and are priced as European, which understates the value of that right a little. With the underlying's daily prices downloaded (and at least 130 days of them) the overview shows the 21- and 63-day realised volatility; without them it says so instead of borrowing a number.
- **Futures.** Yahoo's contract histories are stacked into a curve by expiry date. The expiry dates are computed from each exchange's rule (the third-last business day for gold, the third Friday for the equity indexes, the last business day of the month before delivery for heating oil, and so on) without a holiday calendar, so one can be a day or two off. The feed has **no open interest**, so the open-interest chart and crossover are left out and say why. Margins are the illustrative figures of the model for the five roots the platform simulates (CL, NG, GC, HG, ES); for every other root they are **an illustrative share of the contract's value** by asset class (9% for energy, 6% for metals, agriculture and equity indexes, 3.5% for currencies, 2% for rates, 15% for Bitcoin and Ether), labelled as such in the notes: the exchanges set the real figures and change them with volatility. Deribit's futures carry open interest and volume. The History view reads an iTick product (see above).
- **Crypto.** A downloaded coin gets its own history, funding and the real venues: the overview, funding, basis and liquidity views read them and say which numbers are data. The cross-exchange view compares venues only within one kind of market (spot with spot, perpetual with perpetual), on real bids and asks at the moment of the download, net of entry-level taker fees that are **assumptions** (they depend on your volume and change). Liquidity walks the real order book (up to 400 levels a side, within 5% of the mid) for the cost of $100 thousand, $1 million and $10 million; a size the saved book cannot fill is shown as n/a, not extrapolated. OKX keeps about three months of funding history whatever you ask. Bitcoin and Ether keep the platform's longer Deribit history; the card adds their venues and funding. A history from iTick is spot only, so the basis and funding views have nothing from it.

## What this data is not

- **Delayed or unaudited, and somebody else's service.** Cboe's quotes are 15 minutes late, Yahoo's last bar can be a session still in progress, and a provider can change a field, refuse an address or disappear. Use the data for research on your own machine; each provider's terms of use are theirs. It is not a feed to trade on, and a backtest on it is still a backtest.
- **Not a history of implied volatility.** A chain is one day. The overview's volatility rank needs a year of implied volatility the downloads do not have, so it says "n/a" instead of borrowing the model's.
- **Not the venues you may expect.** Binance and Bybit refuse US and many cloud addresses (HTTP 451 and 403) and CME's site forbids scripts, so none of them is used.
- **Not the P&L of holding a contract.** An iTick futures history is a price series of a product; Yahoo's curves are the prices of single contracts. Neither includes margin, financing or the cost of rolling, and the series from iTick may step on roll days.
- **Shared cloud hosts get refused.** Yahoo and the exchanges rate-limit addresses that many people share, and Replit's is one of them. When a provider answers "HTTP 403" or "HTTP 429", the card says so by name and the app waits a while before asking that host again instead of hammering it. Try again in a few minutes, download fewer items at a time, or run the dashboard on your own computer.

## Where the code is

`src/assets/free_data/` holds one module per tab (`options.py`, `futures.py`, `crypto.py`), the HTTP client (`http.py`: paced per host, retried with a back-off, a cool-down after a refusal, readable errors), the store (`store.py`), the list of sources (`sources.py`) and iTick's bundled product lists (`itick_catalog.py` with `itick_futures.csv` and `itick_crypto.csv`, copies of iTick's own sheets). The iTick downloads themselves are `src/webapp/data_itick.py`, built on the Backtest tab's `ITickProvider` (`src/webapp/providers.py`). The web side is `src/webapp/data_api.py`:

- `GET /api/data/status`: what is downloaded and with which provenance, what each tab can ask for, whether iTick is set up and how many of its calls are free now, and which downloads are running.
- `GET /api/data/search?kind=futures&q=nasdaq&region=US&group=Energy`: products from iTick's bundled list (or `kind=crypto` for coins), best match first. It makes no call.
- `POST /api/data/fetch` (a job; poll `/api/job/<id>`): `{"kind": "options" | "futures" | "crypto", "items": [...], ...settings}`; `"source": "itick"` on a futures or crypto request, `"what": ["chain", "prices"]` on an options one. The browser names a ticker, a root, a coin or a product code; every address and file name is fixed on the server, so a request cannot reach another host or another file. One download per tab at a time, on a worker of its own, so a slow feed never delays a backtest.
- `POST /api/data/delete`: `{"kind", "name", "confirm": true}`.
- `GET /api/futures/series?name=US_NQ`: the History view's numbers for a downloaded product.

The tests replace the network with a table of answers in the shape of the live feeds (`tests/free_data_helpers.py`) and iTick with a fake service (`tests/fake_itick.py`), so none of them goes to the internet. See also [the dashboard guide](dashboard.md), and the guides to [option chains](techniques/option-chains-and-greeks.md), [futures curves](techniques/futures-curves-carry-and-rolls.md) and [crypto funding and venues](techniques/crypto-funding-basis-and-venues.md).

After an update, stop the app and press Run again (on Replit, Stop and then Run): the cards need the app's API version 6, and an older app says so instead of leaving the card empty.
